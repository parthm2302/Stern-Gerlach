Theory

A particle with magnetic moment μ in a magnetic field with a gradient feels a force along the field axis. For a field along z:

Fz = μz · ∂Bz/∂z

Classically μz could take any value, so the beam would smear into a continuous band. Stern and Gerlach (1922) saw instead discrete spots: the component of spin along the measurement axis is quantised, Saxis = mħ with m = −s, −s+1, …, +s, giving 2s+1 beams (two for s = 1/2).

If the incoming spin points along a direction making angle γ with the measurement axis, the probability of each outcome follows from the Born rule. For s = 1/2:

P(up) = cos²(γ/2) = (1 + cos γ)/2     P(down) = sin²(γ/2) = (1 − cos γ)/2

For higher spin (a spin pointing along a definite direction, a "spin-coherent state") the outcomes follow a binomial distribution, P(m) = C(2s, s+m) · ps+m (1−p)s−m with p = cos²(γ/2). An unpolarised beam gives all 2s+1 outcomes with equal probability.

Things to try
  • Spin +z measured along z: 100% in the top beam. Same spin measured along x: 50/50, i.e. measuring along x "forgets" the z information.
  • Increase N and watch the observed % converge to the prediction (fluctuations scale like 1/√N).
  • Set a custom θ = 60°, measure along z: P(up) = cos²(30°) = 75%.
  • Switch on "Show classical prediction" to compare with the continuous smear expected from classical physics.
